# Field EDA — 30-field sample (Assignment 2)

A committed, reproducible run of [`eda-field-sample`](../../GUIDE.md) over three
ten-field farms in Illinois, Iowa, and Nebraska. Every figure and table below is
regenerated by one command from the runtime data; nothing is hand-edited.

The point of the exercise is comparison at three levels: **within a field over
time** (CDL), **across fields within a grower** (geometry and crop mix), and
**across growers** (Illinois vs Iowa vs Nebraska).

## The skill

**`eda-field-sample`** is the custom field-level EDA skill for this assignment.
It lives at `my-farm-advisor/eda/eda-field-sample/` and its workflow is
documented in [`GUIDE.md`](../../GUIDE.md). It reads the canonical runtime farm
data and compares field boundaries, five years of CDL crop history, and
farm-level weather at field, field-year, grower, and across-grower level.

- **Generates:** 19 static PNG figures, 6 CSV tables, and a `manifest.json`
  carrying each caption, sample size, and input checksum.
- **Saves to:** `${DATA_PIPELINE_DATA_ROOT}/data-pipeline/eda/field-sample/`
  (`output/`, `tables/`, `manifest.json`). The copies in this folder are that
  run, committed as-is.
- **Report:** [`output/field-eda-report.pdf`](output/field-eda-report.pdf), a
  one-time assembly of the same manifest and tables.

- Boundaries: USDA Common Land Unit / CSB where available, OpenStreetMap

- Cropland: USDA NASS Cropland Data Layer, five years.
- Weather: NASA POWER daily series, one 0.5° grid cell per farm.

## Reproduce

```bash
export DATA_PIPELINE_DATA_ROOT=$HOME/my-farm-advisor-runtime
cd my-farm-advisor/eda/eda-field-sample
"$DATA_PIPELINE_DATA_ROOT/data-pipeline/.venv/bin/python" scripts/run_field_eda.py
```

A full run writes 19 figures, 6 tables, and `manifest.json` to
`${DATA_PIPELINE_DATA_ROOT}/data-pipeline/eda/field-sample/`. The images and
tables committed here are copies of that run. Add `--no-basemap` for an offline
render; the maps fall back to plain backgrounds and say so in the subtitle.

## Report (one-time assembly)

[`output/field-eda-report.pdf`](output/field-eda-report.pdf) — a 10-page PDF that
walks through the dataset scope and grower locations, the layers used, the four
comparison levels, every figure with the caption recorded in this run, the eight
maps, and the limitations. It was assembled once from this run's `manifest.json`,
tables, and committed PNGs by a throwaway script, so the reusable workflow in
`src/` still ends at the manifest. Regenerating it is not part of the workflow.

Soil analysis was not required for this assignment and was not attempted: no
soil layer is loaded, joined, or mapped anywhere in the report.

## Boundaries

### A1 — Field size distribution

![Field size distribution by grower](output/a1_field_size_distribution.png)

Nebraska's ten fields sit in a 208–458 acre band, while Illinois runs 18–619
acres and Iowa 0.84–326. The sub-acre and sub-20-acre ends are OpenStreetMap
parcels, including a 0.84-acre Iowa sliver, and are kept rather than trimmed.

### A2 — Edge density

![Edge density by grower](output/a2_edge_density.png)

Typical fields need ~23 m of boundary per acre; the 0.84-acre Iowa parcel needs
330 m, about 14× more. Any per-acre cost or sampling metric has to exclude that
parcel or report it separately.

### A3 — Size vs compactness

![Field size vs compactness](output/a3_size_vs_compactness.png)

Across all 30 fields the rank correlation between area and compactness is
−0.28 (p = 0.13), and no subgroup — CSB only, OSM only, or any single grower —
separates from zero. The two least compact fields are mid-sized Illinois CSB
strips, while the single largest field is one of the roundest; size alone does
not predict shape.

## CDL / cropland

### B1 — Crop composition

![Crop composition by grower and year](output/b1_crop_composition.png)

Corn holds 50–68% of Illinois area and 47–59% of Iowa area in every year, while
Nebraska sits at 89–99% except 2022, when soybeans take 60% of its area. That
single year is the only break in a corn-dominated sample.

### B2 — Five-year field sequences

![Dominant crop per field](output/b2_crop_sequence_grid.png)

Field-by-field sequences show Illinois and Iowa alternating on a two-year
rhythm while Nebraska's ten fields are corn in 41 of 50 field-years. The hatched
cell is the 0.84-acre Iowa parcel, which covers too few 30 m pixels to resolve a
2024 class; it is left as "no data", not imputed.

### B3 — Transition rates

![Crop transitions by grower](output/b3_crop_transitions.png)

After a corn year, Illinois and Iowa plant soybean 81% and 76% of the time;
Nebraska only 26%. Corn-to-corn is 0.74 in Nebraska against 0.19 and 0.24 in
Illinois and Iowa.

### B4 — Dominant class counts

![Dominant CDL class counts by grower](output/b4_crop_class_counts.png)

Nebraska is corn-dominant in 41 of 50 field-years, against 27 of 50 in Illinois
and 25 of 49 in Iowa, where soybean accounts for most of the remainder. Iowa's
six non-crop field-years all belong to two small parcels, not to a broader shift
out of crops.

### B5 — Size vs crop-class diversity

![Field size vs crop-class diversity](output/b5_size_vs_diversity.png)

Counting every reported crop class, larger fields look richer: pooled rank
correlation +0.50 (p = 0.005), positive in all three growers (+0.42 IL,
+0.25 IA, +0.47 NE). Requiring a class to cover at least 5% of the field flips
the pooled sign to −0.28 (p = 0.14), with Iowa at −0.80. A larger field
resolves more 30 m classes, so the raw count is partly a resolution artifact;
this sample does not establish an agronomic size–diversity effect.

## Weather

Weather is farm-level: all ten fields in a farm share one NASA POWER grid cell,
so the effective sample is **3 locations × 5 years = 15 farm-years**, never 300
field-days. Because all ten field records carry the same daily values, there is
no within-grower comparison *across fields*; the seasons are that comparison.

### C1 — Monthly temperature

![Monthly temperature climatology](output/c1_monthly_temperature.png)

Nebraska is 1.2 °C warmer than Illinois and Iowa on average and the gap holds
in all twelve months, while Illinois and Iowa stay within a few tenths of a
degree of each other. The band shows year-to-year spread inside each monthly
mean.

### C2 — Growing-season precipitation

![Growing-season precipitation](output/c2_season_precip.png)

Nebraska averages 448 mm over the five seasons against 500 mm in Illinois and
517 mm in Iowa, but Iowa's own range is 331 mm and Nebraska's wettest season
(585 mm) beats every Illinois season. Five seasons per grower cannot rank these
climates.

### C3 — Heat vs water

![Seasonal heat vs seasonal water](output/c3_gdd_vs_precip.png)

The fifteen farm-years separate mainly by heat: Nebraska's mean is 2,069 GDD
against 1,763 in Illinois and 1,882 in Iowa. Inside Iowa and Nebraska the hotter
season is the drier one (rho −0.90 each); Illinois is essentially flat at −0.20.
With five seasons the sign is the finding, not the slope.

## Maps

Farm maps use each farm's local UTM zone; the all-farm maps use three panels at
one shared window (CONUS Albers). Tick labels count metres from each panel's SW
corner; there is no scale bar. Corn frequency uses a discrete 0/5–5/5 legend.

### All farms at one scale

![All 30 fields by boundary source](output/map_boundaries_all.png)

![Five-year corn frequency across all fields](output/map_cdl_all.png)

Drawing the three farms at the same scale makes the geometry comparable:
Illinois mixes blocky CSB fields with OSM parcels of very different sizes, Iowa
carries the sub-acre sliver, and Nebraska's fields are regular rectangular
blocks. Eight of ten Nebraska fields are corn in at least four of five years,
while Illinois and Iowa scatter across the range.

## Manifest and report handoff

The run also writes `manifest.json` next to `output/` and `tables/` in the
runtime tree. It carries each figure's caption, sample size, and the exact
statistics quoted above, plus SHA-256 fingerprints of every input table. The
reusable workflow stops at that manifest on purpose; the one-time report above
consumes it and is not regenerated by the skill.
